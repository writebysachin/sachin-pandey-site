I used to think of search as a simple process.

You have a question.

You open Google.

You type something.

You click a result.

Done.

But that's not always how I search anymore.

If I'm researching something, I might start with Google, then open Reddit to see what people are saying. I might watch a YouTube video. I might ask ChatGPT to explain something differently.

The search doesn't end with one result.

It becomes a process.

And that made me rethink how businesses should approach content.

## The Search Journey Is Bigger Than the Search Box

Think about the last time you seriously researched something before buying it.

Maybe you wanted a new laptop.

You could search Google for the best options. Then you might look at reviews on YouTube. You might search Reddit for problems people have experienced. You might ask an AI tool to compare two products.

Finally, you might visit the company's website.

None of those steps are exactly the same.

But they're all part of the same decision.

Research.

That's the part I think businesses need to pay more attention to.

## Your Customer May Not Discover You Through Your Website

A company can have a great website and still be difficult to discover.

That's because the website is only one possible destination.

People may discover a business through:

- A search result
- An AI answer
- A Reddit discussion
- A YouTube video
- A LinkedIn post
- A recommendation from someone they trust
- A community conversation

The important question isn't whether every business needs to be on all of these.

It doesn't.

The important question is:

> Where is your customer already looking?

That question changes how you think about content.

## This Changes How I Think About Content

Instead of creating content simply because there is a keyword to target, I'm increasingly interested in starting with questions.

- What is the customer trying to understand?
- What are they comparing?
- What are they worried about?
- What would they search before buying?
- What would they ask another person?

Those questions can lead to much better content ideas.

For example, instead of writing another generic article about "SEO tips," a business could answer a much more specific question their customers actually have.

That answer could become an article.

Then it could become a short video.

Then a LinkedIn post.

Then perhaps a useful contribution to a relevant community.

The point isn't to copy and paste the same thing everywhere.

It's to take one useful idea and make it useful in different places.

## I'm Still Testing This

This is something I'm exploring while building Yoga Write Code.

I'm not trying to claim that I have a perfect system.

I'm still learning what works.

I'm looking at how people search, how they discover businesses, and how content can help a business become easier to find.

Some ideas will work.

Some won't.

I'll probably change my mind about some of this as I test more.

And that's exactly why I'm documenting it here.

## The Question I'm Asking Now

For a long time, the question was:

> "How do I get more traffic?"

I'm starting to think there's a better question:

> "How does my customer find me when they need what I offer?"

Those questions sound similar.

They're not.

The first focuses on a metric.

The second focuses on the person.

And I think that's a much better place to start.
