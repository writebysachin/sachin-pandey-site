For a long time, when I thought about search, I thought about Google.

Find a keyword. Create a page. Optimize it. Build authority. Try to rank.

That model still matters.

But the way I personally search has started to change.

When I want to understand something, I don't always open Google first.

Sometimes I ask ChatGPT.

Sometimes I search Reddit to see what real people are saying.

Sometimes I watch YouTube.

Sometimes I look through LinkedIn.

And sometimes I still use Google.

That made me start thinking about something:

**What happens when search isn't just one place anymore?**

## Search is becoming part of a bigger research journey

Imagine someone wants to buy a piece of software.

They might start with Google.

Then they might search Reddit for people's experiences.

They might watch a YouTube review.

They might ask an AI tool to compare a few options.

Then they might visit the company's website before making a decision.

The search didn't happen in one place.

It happened across several places.

And I think this is an important shift for businesses to understand.

The question isn't only:

> "How do I rank on Google?"

A better question might be:

> **"Where does my customer look before making a decision?"**

## This doesn't mean SEO is dead

I'm not convinced that the answer is to throw away SEO.

In fact, I think that's the wrong conclusion.

Google is still an important way people discover information.

Websites still matter.

Content still matters.

Technical foundations still matter.

But I think the definition of "search" is getting bigger.

A customer doesn't necessarily care whether the information they need comes from a search engine, an AI assistant, a Reddit discussion, a YouTube video, or a company's website.

They care about finding a useful answer they can trust.

That changes how I think about content.

## Publishing isn't the whole job

One thing I've been thinking about is the difference between **creating content** and **making content discoverable**.

You can write a really good article.

But if nobody who needs it finds it, the article doesn't do much.

That means distribution matters.

A single idea could become:

- A detailed article
- A LinkedIn post
- An Instagram post
- A short video
- A Reddit discussion
- A newsletter
- An answer to a question someone is already asking

I'm not saying every piece of content needs to be everywhere.

That would quickly become another pointless content treadmill.

The better approach is to understand where your audience already spends time and create useful content for those places.

## What I currently believe

At this point, I have a few working beliefs.

### 1. Search is becoming more fragmented

People have more places to look for information than they did before.

That creates more opportunities, but it also makes discovery harder to think about.

### 2. Useful content matters more than simply producing more content

Publishing ten average articles isn't necessarily better than publishing one genuinely useful piece of work.

I'd rather create something that actually answers a question.

### 3. Original experience is valuable

There is already an enormous amount of recycled marketing advice online.

If I write about something I've actually tested, built, measured, or experienced, I have something more interesting to contribute.

### 4. SEO isn't disappearing

I don't think businesses should abandon SEO because AI search exists.

I think they should understand how SEO fits into a much larger discovery journey.

### 5. We still don't know exactly where this is going

This is probably the most important one.

AI search is changing quickly.

Some things that seem important today may not matter as much later.

So I'm less interested in declaring a new "perfect strategy" and more interested in testing what actually works.

- Does publishing across multiple channels actually create better discovery?

## My current takeaway

I don't think the future of search is simply:

**Google vs AI.**

I think it's more complicated.

People will continue using different tools for different parts of their research.

Google might help them find websites.

Reddit might help them understand people's experiences.

YouTube might help them see something in action.

AI might help them understand or compare information.

And a company's own website might ultimately be where they decide whether they trust the business.

So instead of asking only:

**"How do I rank?"**

I'm starting with a different question:

**"Where does my customer look before they choose?"**

That's the question I'm exploring while building Yoga Write Code.

And I'll share what I learn here.
